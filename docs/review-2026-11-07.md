### 2. The servo hatch: options wanted

**Now:** a hatch is its own part (SB1-H), laid out on fresh sheet and weighed, while the sheet over the bay has no opening in it. So the hatch is cut and weighed twice. Neither of the two fixes offered (the hatch as the offcut, or the hatch as a separate piece) is right everywhere, and a foam wing always needs a new piece, because routing the pocket destroys the skin over it.

**Proposed:** cut the opening in the sheet in every case, and give each bay with a hatch a **Hatch made from** setting:

- **The piece cut out** (built-up wings only). The cut list notes the hatch as the offcut of its sheet; nothing extra is laid out or weighed. Grain and thickness match the skin.
- **A new piece of the skin's sheet.** Its own part, laid out with the sheeting (or a foam wing's skins), same material and thickness as the skin. The only choice on a foam wing, and its default.
- **A new piece of another material.** Its own part with a material and thickness of its own (ply, say), for a hatch that takes screws.

Questions for Matt and Sam:

- Is that the right set, and what should a built-up wing default to?
- Does a hatch want a rebate or a frame under it besides the rails already listed, and should a new piece be cut a little oversize to trim?
- On a foam wing, is the filler piece glued back flush once the servo is in (so it's really a patch, not a hatch), or kept removable?

### 3. Thin ribs: per-rib choices, and where the caps stop

Two answers from 14 November belong together, and Matt wants to explore them properly before anything is built.

**Per-rib Split or Thin choices (was 5).** Each rib too thin for the spar has its own choice, saved against the rib's name, so adding a rib moves the choice to a different rib; and caps and solid spars share the saved choices with different meanings. Neither fix offered (save by distance, or clear on change) was accepted.

**"If the rib is too thin for the cap, do not add a cap until the rib is thick enough to thin for it" (was 8).** Confirmed by Matt on 14 November: working in from the tip, the spar caps start at the first rib deep enough to take a cap (thinned if need be) with the rib's margin left between them. Outboard of that there are no caps at all: the ribs are whole, and the strength check treats that stretch as having no spar, as it does beyond a short tube. That would replace today's behaviour, where caps always run to the tip and are thinned or the rib is split, and it would make the "cap thickness needed" figure's flaw moot.

Things to settle when we explore it:

- Is "no cap" a third per-rib choice beside Split and Thin, or a rule that applies by itself wherever a thinned cap would be under some minimum thickness (and what minimum)?
- What carries the load outboard of the caps: sheeting only, or a stringer or reinforcement run on?
- Does the same rule apply to a solid spar that tapers to nothing?
- With caps stopping short, do the per-rib choices still need saving at all, or does one wing-wide rule cover it?
