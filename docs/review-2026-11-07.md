## A. Building questions (yours or Sam's call)

### 1. Which hinges do we model besides Mylar tabs?

**Now:** "Pinned" is a Robart-style hinge point: a pin of a set diameter and length lying *across* the hinge line, glued half into each hinge spar. The 3D view draws each as a short bar across the hinge, and there are no tabs. You said the pins "run the wrong way" and that a catalogue of parts is needed.

**The question:** what did you expect to see?

- **A. Hinge points across the hinge (as now), from a catalogue.** Robart hinge points in their real sizes, each with its drill size and depth, picked from a list instead of typed as diameter and length.
- **B. A pin along the hinge line.** A piano hinge, or a wire through tube knuckles: one long pin, with leaves or knuckles let into each spar. This is a different hinge and would be a new type beside the current one.
- **C. Both**, as separate types.

**I'd do:** A now (small: a catalogue and a picker), and B only if you or Sam build that way. Either way I need the makes and sizes you actually use.

### 2. The servo hatch is counted twice

**Now:** a servo hatch is its own part (SB1-H), laid out on fresh sheet in the DXF and weighed. Its note says it's cut from the sheeting over the bay, but that sheet part has no opening in it. So the hatch's area is weighed twice and cut twice.

- **A. The hatch is the offcut.** Cut the opening in the sheet part; the hatch stays in the cut list as a note ("the piece cut from S3"), with no layout of its own and no extra weight. Grain and thickness match the skin automatically.
- **B. The hatch is a separate piece.** Cut the opening in the sheet part *and* keep the hatch as its own part on fresh stock. Right if the hatch is a different material or thickness from the skin (ply, say).

**I'd do:** A, unless hatches are usually ply. This applies to foam wings' skins too.

### 3. A sheeted hard point on an open wing has no material setting

**Now:** a hard point's sheet takes the wing's sheeting material. On an open-structure wing the Sheeting section's material picker is hidden, so the hard point is cut from a material you can't see or change (medium balsa unless an earlier setting is left over).

- **A. Give each sheeted hard point its own material picker**, defaulting to the wing's sheeting material.
- **B. Show the wing's sheeting material whenever any hard point is sheeted.**

**I'd do:** A. A hard point is often ply where the skin is balsa.

### 4. Cap strip material can't be set

**Now:** cap strips are listed and drawn as medium balsa. The setting exists in the saved project but no control changes it.

- **A. Add a material picker** beside the cap strip width and thickness.
- **B. Leave it fixed** and remove the setting.

**I'd do:** B unless anyone uses anything but balsa for cap strips.

### 5. Per-rib Split or Thin choices move when ribs are added

**Now:** where a rib is too thin for the spar, each such rib has its own choice (split the rib, or thin the caps / taper the spar). The choice is saved against the rib's name (R12). Add a rib inboard and R12 is a different rib, so the choice lands on the wrong one. Also, caps spars and solid spars share the saved choices, and the same stored value means "thin the caps" for one and "taper the spar" for the other, so switching spar type carries choices over with a changed meaning.

- **A. Save each choice against the rib's distance along the span**, so it stays with the place. A rib that moves away loses its choice and falls back to the wing-wide setting.
- **B. Keep names, but clear the per-rib choices** when ribs are added or removed or the spar type changes, and say so.

**I'd do:** A, and clear on a change of spar type either way.

### 6. A foam wing's hidden spar position still places things

**Now:** a foam wing has no spar, so the Spar section is hidden. But the spar's chord position is still the line that hinges, servo bays and air brakes are placed against. It can't be moved on a foam wing, and a surface set too far forward is told it "would run into the main spar".

- **A. Show the position on foam wings as a "reference line"** (in Foam core and skins), with the warning reworded for foam.
- **B. Place foam wings' fittings against something real instead**, such as a fixed fraction of the chord or the thickest point, and drop the setting for foam.

**I'd do:** A. It's a few lines and keeps old foam projects as they are.

## B. The strength check (how careful should it be?)

These four are all cases where the check is slightly optimistic or its advice is slightly off. None changes a healthy wing's result much; together they decide how far the check can be trusted near its limits.

### 7. Hard-point sheets are counted in bending but not themselves checked

**Now:** a sheeted hard point's sheet adds to the wing's bending strength, which takes load off the spar. But (a) it's counted right back to the trailing-edge stock, while the sheet as cut stops at the fixed hinge spar beside a control surface; and (b) the sheet itself is never checked for crushing or buckling on an open wing, only partly on a D-box, and at the wing's sheet thickness rather than its own on a fully sheeted wing.

- **A. Count it only where it's built, and check it** for crushing and buckling at its own thickness wherever it's counted.
- **B. Don't count hard-point sheets in bending at all.** Simpler and on the safe side; a hard point is local reinforcement, not structure to rely on.

**I'd do:** B. You asked on 25 October for them to be counted ("Don't, then?"), so this reverses that; say if you'd rather have A.

### 8. "Cap thickness needed" overstates where caps are thinned

**Now:** where caps are thinned at a thin rib, the "you need caps this thick" figure scales the full thickness by how overstressed the *thinned* cap is. The answer comes out too big, and thicker caps wouldn't help there anyway because they'd be thinned again.

**I'd do:** scale from the thinned thickness, and where the caps are being thinned say "split this rib instead". Yes or no?

### 9. The spar fit check assumes sheeting over the spar on a D-box

**Now:** on a D-box whose sheeting stops ahead of the spar on one side, the "spar doesn't fit this rib" check still leaves room for sheet over the spar on that side, so it can say a spar doesn't fit when it does.

**I'd do:** check top and bottom separately. Yes or no?

### 10. The round leading edge's "largest rod that fits" is found at the panel ends only

**Now:** the fit of a rod or dowel leading edge is checked at every rib, but the size the warning recommends was worked out at the panel ends, so on a curved or thinning panel it can recommend a rod that doesn't fit mid-panel.

**I'd do:** work the recommendation out over the same ribs. Yes or no?

## C. Editors and layout

### 11. The loads chart still pans with the left button

**Now:** everywhere else the left button selects and never pans; right or middle drag pans. The loads chart has nothing to select, so I left its left-drag pan alone.

- **A. Leave it.** A chart isn't an editor.
- **B. Match the rest**, so one rule holds everywhere.

**I'd do:** A.

### 12. The section editor has no Snap

**Now:** the wing editor and structure plan snap drags to the grid, with a Snap toggle beside the zoom buttons. The section editor has no snapping at all, though the project's own rule is a Snap toggle in every view that edits.

- **A. Add grid snapping** to point and segment drags, with the toggle (Alt to place freely, as elsewhere).
- **B. Record the section editor as exempt.** A section's points are placed by eye against the curvature comb, and a grid may get in the way.

**I'd do:** B, unless you've wanted snapping while drawing a section.

### 13. The servo configurator's warnings point at controls it doesn't have

**Now:** the dialogue lists the linkage's warnings, some of which say "move the horn along the hinge", "turn it to line up", "square the servo to the hinge" or "move the bay". All four controls are in the surface's side panel behind the dialogue, so you have to close it to act.

- **A. Say where they are** in the warning text.
- **B. Bring horn position, horn turn and square-to-hinge into the dialogue.**

**I'd do:** B. They're linkage settings and the dialogue is where the linkage is judged; the bay's centre can stay outside.

### 14. Rib labels follow their own rules

**Now:** rib labels are sized 1.5 to 5 mm where every other part's are 2 to 4 mm ("below 2 mm an engraving is hard to read"); when no place fits, the label is put down at 1.5 mm without checking it's inside the rib; and a label can land in a leading-edge rod's notch.

**I'd do:** same 2 to 4 mm range as other parts, keep clear of the rod notch, and put a label that won't fit in the waste beside the part, as other parts do. Yes or no?
