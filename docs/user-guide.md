# Wingcalc user guide

How to use the app. For how the numbers are worked out, see [Methods](methods.md).

## Entering values

Every input and output works in metric or imperial. The units menu at the top right sets the system and, within it, the unit for lengths (mm or m; in or ft), weights (g or kg; oz or lb) and speeds (km/h or m/s); areas and wing loading follow the length and weight units. The units are kept with the project. Numeric boxes accept sums (`700 - 20`, `(120 + 80) / 2`) and numbers with their own units (`24in + 50mm` in a millimetre box); commas are thousands separators. A percent-of-chord box takes a length too: `12mm` becomes its share of the chord, the root chord for settings that apply to the whole wing (spar position, D-box depth, cap strip start, stringers) and the local chord for a hinge, an air brake or a panel's thickness; the line under the box says which chord it used. A value is taken when you press Enter or leave the box, not while you type; Escape puts back the last value. An unfinished sum leaves the last good value in place, and the result shows under the box while you type. A warning about a value (an unusual weight, span or aspect ratio, a panel's chord or rise) shows under its box.

The wing is entered as fuselage width, root chord, then one or more panels per side, each with its own length, outer chord, sweep and dihedral, and straight edges, Bézier curves or chords dimensioned at stations, plus an optional rounded wingtip. For a quick check, mean chord or area can be entered instead.

The inputs column, the panel fields and the structure settings are foldable sections: click a heading to open or close it, and a closed section shows its key values on its heading line. One section in a column is open at a time: opening one closes the others. Which section is open is remembered in this browser, not in the project.

## Projects

The bar at the top holds the **File** menu, the project name, **Save** and undo/redo on the left, and the units and theme on the right.

- **Save** stores the project in this browser under its name; saving again updates it. A dot in the name box means there are unsaved changes.
- **File** holds **New project** (asking first if there are unsaved changes), **Save as copy**, the saved projects to open (newest first; delete one with its ×), and **Export to file** and **Import from file**.
- **Auto / Light / Dark** sets the colour theme. Auto follows the system setting. The choice is remembered in this browser; it isn't part of a project.
- The work in progress **autosaves** and comes back on reload, saved or not.
- **Export to file** downloads the project as a `.wingcalc.json` file; **Import from file** opens one. Use these to back up or move projects between browsers: saved projects belong to one browser on one machine and are lost if site data is cleared.

A project holds the units, weight, wing shape (including stations, tip, washout, thickness taper and dimensions), the aerofoil choice, the structure and the export options. Files from older versions open with anything missing filled from the defaults. If the browser blocks storage, saving is disabled with a note and Export still works.

## Wing plan editor

In "Chords & panels" mode the results column shows the wing plan: one half of the wing, drawn flat (as built), with the other half ghosted.

**Open wing editor** expands it to fill the browser window: the plan takes most of the space, the wing and panel settings sit on the left (tabs switch between panels), and a strip along the top keeps span, area, aspect ratio, cube loading and stall speed in view as you edit. Done or Esc returns to the page. The same values can be typed in the inputs column, which is the precise route; the plan updates as you type.

- **Select** a panel by clicking it (or its heading in the inputs column); the two stay in step, and selecting a panel opens its section.
- **Drag** the round handles at a panel's tip to change its length and sweep (leading edge) or its tip chord (trailing edge). Hold Shift to change length without changing sweep. The handle on the root trailing edge sets the root chord; the diamond ahead of the root sets the fuselage width.
- **Curves**: on the selected panel, drag the small dashed handle in the middle of either edge to curve it. That creates a square control handle; drag it to shape the curve, double-click it to straighten the edge again.
- **Chord stations**: set a panel's shape to "Chord stations" to define it by chords dimensioned along it. Each station has a distance from the panel's inner end, a chord, and a leading-edge set-back (how far its leading edge sits behind the panel's inner leading edge). Leave a set-back blank ("auto") and the station takes its leading edge from the line through its neighbours.
  - **Add station** under the table adds a blank row just above the tip, ready to type into; it's skipped until it has a distance and chord. **Add station** in the plan's command bar places one on the current shape in the middle of the largest gap, so the outline doesn't jump.
  - **The table** starts with the panel's inner end (greyed: its chord comes from the panel inboard) and ends with its tip, whose distance, chord and set-back are the panel's length, tip chord and sweep.
  - **Distances** are measured from the panel's inner end by default; switch to **Spacing** to enter each as the gap from the row above. In spacing mode, changing a gap moves everything outboard of it, tip included, like chained dimensions on a drawing.
  - **Typing**: Enter moves to the next box, like Tab (Shift+Enter goes back). A row being edited keeps its place until you leave it, then the table re-sorts by distance.
  - **Problems** don't stop the panel drawing. A station before the inner end, on or beyond the tip, or at the same place as another is skipped: its row says why, the warnings list names it, and the plan shows it as a dashed rib in the warning colour.
  - **Keep straight**: choose the leading edge, the trailing edge or a chord line (at a percentage you set; 25% for a straight quarter-chord line) to stay straight from end to end. Stations then set only their chord, and the edges sit either side of the straight line in proportion.
  - **Segments**: between the station rows, each segment shows whether it's curved or straight; click to switch. All straight gives a faceted outline, like straight sheeting between ribs. On the selected panel, drag a station's leading-edge handle to move it along the panel and set its set-back, and its trailing-edge handle to change its chord. Switching an existing panel to stations samples its current shape, so nothing changes until you edit.
- **Wingtip**: tick "Rounded tip" to add a rounded cap beyond the last panel. Drag its handle outward for the extension and along the chord for where it reaches furthest (0% rakes it back from the leading edge, 100% forward from the trailing edge).
- **Measure** (top left, or M; Esc or a second click stops): drag between two points to place a dimension. Ends snap to the wing's corners, stations, tip and root (a green ring shows the target) or to the grid otherwise; Alt ignores snapping. Ends snapped to the wing follow it as it changes. Hold Shift for a purely spanwise or chordwise dimension. Click a dimension and press Delete to remove it, or use **Clear dimensions**. Dimensions are saved with the project.
- **Snap** to the grid is on by default; the grid adapts to the zoom, and Alt disables snapping during a drag.
- **Navigate**: scroll to zoom about the pointer, drag empty space or a panel to pan; − + Fit sit in the bottom-right corner (F, + and − while the plan has focus). The view re-fits when the wing changes from outside the editor, but not during your own drags.
- **Keyboard** (with the plan focused): arrow keys move the selected panel's tip leading edge one grid step (left/right for length, up/down for sweep), Alt with up/down changes its tip chord, Delete removes the selected panel or dimension, M turns Measure on and off, Escape cancels, stops measuring or deselects.
- **Add panel** (in the bar along the top when nothing is selected) appends a new section at the tip, continuing the leading-edge line and dihedral of the last panel at about 40% of its length and 75% of its tip chord.
- **Insert after** (or + on a panel's fields) puts a new constant-chord panel just outboard of the selected one, so the panels further out keep their shape and the wing gets longer.
- **← / →** move the selected panel inward or outward. Each panel keeps its own shape; only the order changes.
- **Control surfaces**: with a panel selected, **Add control surface** in the bar along the top puts one in the panel's longest free stretch: the wing's first is an aileron in the outer part of it, later ones flaps. A new surface is as deep as the trailing edge stock chosen in the structure editor, so the stock itself is the surface, and it keeps that depth at both ends, parallel to the trailing edge, as you move its ends along the span, until you move its hinge by hand. Surfaces are drawn tinted from the hinge line (dashed) back to the trailing edge; click one to select it, and Delete (or the bar) removes it. A selected surface has a handle at each end of its hinge line: drag it along the span to move that end, and along the chord to set the depth there (Shift moves it along the span only). The handle in the middle of the hinge line moves the whole hinge forward or back. Its depth at each end, its length and how far it sits from the panel's inner end are shown beside it: click any of them to type it (Enter or clicking away sets it, Escape leaves it). Typing a depth stops the surface following the trailing edge. A surface that overlaps another is drawn in the warning colour and isn't used.
- **Air brakes**: with a panel selected, **Add air brake** in the bar puts a blade brake near the middle of the panel (a pop-up spoiler is added from the inspector). Each is drawn as its box or plate on the panel; click one to select it, and Delete (or the bar) removes it. One whose ribs would sit on another's is drawn in the warning colour and isn't used.
- **Undo/Redo** (in the header, or Ctrl+Z / Ctrl+Y) covers the wing shape, the dimensions, the control surfaces and the structure: each drag is one step, and typed changes are grouped once they pause. Opening a project starts a fresh history.

**Dihedral** can be entered per panel as an angle or as the **rise** of the panel's outer end above its inner end with the wing level (for a polyhedral tip panel, measured from the break). This isn't the "block the tip up X" figure on some plans, where the inner panel is pinned flat: for a tip panel that gives the angle relative to the inner panel, so add the inner panel's angle.

**Control surfaces** are listed under **Control surfaces**, below the panels (in the inputs column and in the wing editor), one foldable section each, named by role and order from the root (A1, A2 for ailerons, F1 for flaps, FA1 for flaperons). Opening one selects it in the plan, and the other way round. Each has:

- **Role**: aileron, flap or flaperon. Changing it sets that role's usual throws.
- **On**, **From** and **To**: the panel it's on, and where it starts and ends, measured from that panel's inner end. A surface stays on one panel, because a hinge has to be straight; use one each side of a break.
- **Hinge, inner end** and **outer end**: where the hinge line is, as a percentage of the chord back from the leading edge (75% makes a surface a quarter of the chord). It runs straight between the two, so it can taper. Typing either, or dragging the hinge in the plan, stops the surface following the trailing edge.
- **Most up** and **Most down**: the throws, trailing edge up and down. The surface's leading edge (its hinge spar, or the stock itself) is bevelled to at least each throw.
- The surface's **hinge gap** and **servo bay** are structure, so they're set in the structure editor, under **Control surfaces**.

**Add control surface to panel N** adds one to the panel selected in the plan (or the last panel). **Crow** (ailerons up, flaps down together, for steep slow landings) is offered only when the wing has separate ailerons and flaps and no flaperons; tick it to set how far each goes. Each surface goes no further than its own throw. **Landing flap** (shown when there's a flap or flaperon) sets how far they go down for the stall speed with flaps; it starts at the flaps' most down.

**Configuration**, under the stall speed (in "Chords & panels" mode, with a flap or flaperon), switches the stall speed and stall character between **Clean**, **Flaps** (at the landing flap setting) and **Crow** (when crow is on). Each button shows that configuration's stall speed; hover for where its stall starts. Clean is the default, and what the structure is checked for. With flaps or crow chosen, the note under the stall speed lists the deflections and the wing CLmax, and says whether each deflected surface's effect comes from XFOIL (the section solved with it deflected, a second or two after the clean polar) or is estimated from flap theory; the spanwise lift chart shows that configuration's lift at the stall. See [methods](methods.md#flaps-and-crow).

**Air brakes** are listed under **Air brakes**, just below the control surfaces, one foldable section each, named from the root (AB1, AB2). There are two kinds. **Lattice blades** are Schempp-Hirth style: one or more blades that rise vertically out of a box in the wing through slots in the covering. A **pop-up spoiler** is a plate hinged along its front edge on the top surface, lifting at the back. Each has:

- **Type**, and the panel it's **On**.
- **Centre, along the span** (from the panel's inner end, flat along it) and **Length**. A rib goes at each end, a little further apart than the length (1.5 mm each end for blades, 6 mm for a spoiler's frame).
- **Position on the chord** (percent of the chord back from the leading edge, to the middle of the box or plate) and **Box width** (blades) or **Plate chord** (spoiler).
- Blades: **Blade height** (how far each rises above the skin), how many **Blades** (one is a single blade; more stand side by side across the box as a lattice) and whether they go through the **top only** or **top and bottom**.
- A spoiler: **Opens to**, the angle the plate lifts to.
- **Servo bay**: every brake has one unless you untick it, with the same **Servo** class and **Bay centre** choices as a control surface. By default it is the bay beside the brake on its inboard side, sharing the brake's end rib (outboard of it if there's no room inboard).

A brake that can't be built is left out, and the section says why. Warnings (in the structure editor) say where a brake doesn't fit: blades that need more depth than the wing has there (blades through both skins need twice their height), a box that crosses the main spar or runs into the edge stock or a control surface's hinge spar beside it, a brake in the way of a servo bay, or one that would block the lead holes of a bay further out. The brake is still built, so you can see it; move it or change its size. Air brakes' drag isn't calculated yet.

**Washout and thickness taper** are set per panel: washout is the total twist at a panel's outer end relative to the root (nose down), varying evenly across the panel; thickness is the section thickness at the outer end, blank to keep the inner end's. The section drawing shows the tip section dashed over the root.

## Aerofoils

Aerofoils are chosen from a catalogue of the whole UIUC Airfoil Coordinates Database in NACA, Eppler and Other tabs; 78 popular sections also have bundled polars. Multi-element sets (a slat, main element and flap together, such as 30P-30N) aren't listed, as nothing here can model them, but an element the database has as a file of its own is. A project saved with one before it was left out opens with a warning on the section drawing and the structure card, and no aerofoil figures, until another section is chosen. A **custom section** can be defined by its thickness and camber (each with its position) and its leading-edge radius, and is drawn to true proportion with its chord line, mean line and leading-edge circle so it can be checked by eye.

Results appear in two stages: instantly from the bundled polars or an estimate, then a second or two later from an XFOIL polar computed in the browser at the stall's own Reynolds number. The lift curve is drawn with the peak and the stall band marked, so the stall rating can be checked by eye. See [Methods](methods.md#polars-computed-in-the-browser-xfoil).

### Section editor

On a custom section, **Advanced** opens the section editor: the upper and lower surfaces drawn full-window, as smooth curves through control points. It starts from the section as the thickness and camber fields describe it, or from the drawn section already in use, so you can start from any section. Undo and Redo are in the header.

- **Drag** a point to move it. Hold **Shift** while dragging to lock the move to one axis. **Shift-click** adds a point to the selection.
- **Box select**: drag on empty space to select several points, then drag them together or use the command bar (Smooth, Mirror, Delete).
- **Right or middle drag** pans. The **−** and **+** buttons (bottom right, or the keys) zoom, and **F** fits the drawing.
- **Double-click** on a segment adds a point there.
- **Segments** between points are straight or curved. Select one and use **Make straight** or **Make curved**; **Bend** pulls a curved segment, and dragging a straight one moves it. The nose end is set by the radius, so the segment there can't be made straight.
- **Nose radius**: the diamond along the chord sets the leading-edge radius, and the side panel takes a typed value. The outline has exactly that radius at the nose.
- **Smooth** fairs the selected points (or, from the menu, the upper surface, the lower surface or both) so the curvature varies smoothly. Press again for more. **Keep max thickness** rescales the thickness afterwards, since fairing shrinks the section a little.
- **Reset** (the menu in the command bar) goes back to the start of this edit, or to a built-in section. Undo brings the shape back.
- **Trailing edge**: always sharp. The two surfaces end on one point, and dragging either end moves both. The thickness you build comes from the trailing-edge stock in the structure editor.
- **Drawing in** (side panel): % chord, or **Millimetres**, with the length one unit of the drawing stands for (the root chord to start with). In millimetres the grid and rulers read mm, the selected point's position can be typed in mm as drawn, and the readouts give each size in mm too: useful for tracing a real rib. **Normalise** scales the drawing to a unit chord and goes back to % chord.
- The grid and rulers have the same spacing on both axes. The wheel zooms once the drawing has been clicked; before that it scrolls the page.
- **1×** and **3×** show the drawing at true proportion or with the thickness stretched three times, to see small bumps.
- **Comb** draws the curvature as a comb along the outline: each tooth is proportional to the curvature, on the side it bends toward. A smooth outline has a comb that varies gently.
- **Camber and markers** shows the mean line and the thickness and camber positions.
- **Curvature check** (side panel): a surface whose curvature reverses twice is listed with where it is, and a warning says if the two surfaces cross over. Click the warning to select the points either side.
- **XFOIL preview** (side panel): about a second after an edit settles (never during a drag), the outline is solved by XFOIL at the Reynolds number the wing uses, and its lift curve is drawn with Clmax and the stall angle. The section as it was when you opened the editor is drawn faintly for comparison. "Solving" means the result is still coming; "Out of date" means you are still dragging. If XFOIL can't converge on the shape, the preview says so. It runs in the background and doesn't hold up the editor.
- **Use section** writes the drawn section to the aerofoil, and **Cancel** (or Escape) closes the editor, asking first if there are changes not yet used.

The drawn section then appears in the aerofoil picker in place of the custom parameters, with **Edit** to reopen the editor and **Back to parameters** to return to the thickness and camber fields. The drawn section is saved with the project.

## Structure editor

In "Chords & panels" mode a **Structure and ribs** card sums up the structure: small drawings of the plan and the wing in 3D, then the spar, covering, rib count and spacing, the lowest margin of safety at the design load, the root bending moment and an estimate of the wing's weight, structure and covering, with the covering's share.

**Open structure editor** fills the window: settings on the left (each warning at the top of the section whose settings fix it, with a count on the section's heading; warnings about servo bays and air brakes, which are set in the wing editor, are listed under the settings), and on the right, cards for the plan with every rib, a 3D view, the loads along the span and a preview of any part. The expand arrows on the Plan and 3D cards grow that card to most of the window's height, and back. **Export…** at the right of the top strip opens the export options.

### What you set

- **Construction**, at the top: **Built-up** (ribs on a spar, with sheeting and covering, as described below) or **Foam core** (see [Foam-core wings](#foam-core-wings)). Switching keeps both sets of settings, so you can compare the two and switch back.
- **Design loads**: load factor (6 g sport, 10 g or more for aerobatics or hard launches), a negative load factor, and a safety factor (1.5 by default) applied on top.
- **Spar**: caps and shear web (one web, flush with the caps' front face), box (two webs, flush with both faces), carbon tube, or a single solid spar (through a slot, or notched into the top or bottom of each rib). Its position is a chord fraction held at every panel end and straight across each panel; blank puts it at the section's thickest point. A tube can stop short of the tip.
- **Covering**: open (ribs only, optional cap strips on each rib), D-box (sheeted from the leading edge back to the spar, cap strips behind it) or fully sheeted. On a D-box the top and bottom are set separately: sheet either or both, each back to the spar or to a percentage of the chord of its own (past the spar, or short of it). The ribs are inset by whatever covers them. Cap strips start a set percentage of the chord back from the leading edge (10% to start with) wherever there's no sheeting ahead of them, so the rib's own nose sets the leading-edge shape. **Covered with** sets the finish over everything: heat-shrink film (lightweight or standard), a covering film such as Solarfilm, Oracover or Doculam, fabric film, tissue and dope, or glass or carbon cloth with epoxy (light, standard or heavy weights) over the sheeting. The hint under it says what the check assumes and what the covering weighs on this wing. Films, fabric and tissue count as weight only; glass and carbon with epoxy also share the bending with the sheeting and stiffen it against buckling between ribs. Glass and carbon go on sheeting only, so a D-box's open bays behind the spar are taken as lightweight film, and on an open wing a warning says it has nothing to go on.
- **Leading and trailing edges**: stock from SLEC's standard range (symmetrical profiles and strip on edge for the leading edge; triangular, or square strip as a bolster with wood right through for hinge slots and strength, for the trailing edge), picked automatically (Auto never picks square) to fit the section or chosen by hand (stock that does not fit at the edge is set past it, the part past the edge carved away, and the fit drawing says by how much), or a custom size: for the leading edge, its depth and its height, checked as a plain block carved to the section. The ribs are cut back by its depth. Each edge's stock is **fitted** square to the rib (its flat back glued to the square-cut rib face) or **turned to the camber** (less carved away; the ribs' faces are then cut to its angle). The leading edge can also be **let into the ribs**: a strip in a notch in each rib's lower front corner, its bottom along the lower surface, the rib running forward over it; it needs at least 1.5 mm of rib over the strip's back corner. A let-in strip can be **laid** on edge, flat, or the best way up (whichever wastes least). Under the edge settings the editor always draws the fit where it's tightest, true to scale: the stock as sold, dashed, where it's placed; what's left once carved; the rib behind its face; and the section. It says where the stock sits, how far it reaches into the wing and how much it has to spare. If the chosen stock is too small to carve the section from, a warning says at which rib and by about how much, and the drawing shows the part it can't reach in red. The leading edge can instead be a **carbon rod** or **hardwood dowel** (by diameter, from the sizes sold), which forms the nose and sits in a half-round notch at the front of each rib, the sheeting or covering meeting it at its centre line; it can't be carved, so the editor says the largest size that fits and a warning says if the chosen one stands proud of the section at any rib, and the editor draws it where it's worst, the proud part in red. Or **none**: a sheeted nose, the ribs running forward to the nose and the sheeting bent round it (it needs a D-box or fully sheeted wing).
- **Stringers**: any number, by chord position, on the top, bottom or both surfaces. Each is notched into every rib and counted in bending; a top stringer just behind the leading edge doubles as a turbulator spar.
- **Ribs**: material and thickness; streamwise or square to the spar (the root, panel-break and tip ribs always stay streamwise so the panels and fuselage meet cleanly); one-piece wing (a centre rib shared by both halves) or two halves plugging in at the fuselage side. Optional **lightening holes** (not in the root, break or tip ribs) and **jig tabs**.
- **Rib spacing**: **from loads** (as close as the structure needs, never wider than a maximum set for the covering), **even**, **tapered** from root to tip, or **manual**. **Hard points** add ribs at set distances (servo bays, aileron ends, air brake boxes). Give a hard point a second distance (**To**) and it becomes a sheeted hard point: a rib there too, and every bay between the two **sheeted** across the whole chord (from the leading edge stock to the trailing edge stock, or to the fixed hinge spar beside a control surface) on the top, the bottom or both (new ones start as both; **Open** leaves them unsheeted). The ribs there are inset by the sheeting, the plan hatches it, the 3D view and its section cut show it, and a template for each sheet is in the export. Switching to manual starts from the ribs as they are, and a panel added while it's on gets ribs evenly spaced at the pitch, as rows in the list (to fill a panel that's missing them, switch to Even and back to Manual); the list shows every rib by its name on the plan (R1 root outward), the root, panel-end, tip and hard-point ribs as fixed rows, keeps its order while you edit it and sorts by distance when you leave it; then double-click the wing to add a rib there, or use **Add rib** in the bar along the top (or A) to add one mid-way along the longest bay; drag a rib or hard point along the span (it snaps to the grid; Alt places it freely), Delete removes the selected one and the arrow keys nudge it (Shift for ten steps). The root, panel-break and tip ribs are fixed.
- **Corner gussets** where ribs meet the leading or trailing edge, on the root, break and tip ribs or on every rib, one side or both, flush with the rib's top edge, bottom edge, or both, chosen separately for each edge. Each is cut to the actual angle between rib and edge. A placement there isn't depth for between the skins is turned off (top and bottom needs twice the gusset's thickness, which a thin trailing edge seldom has), and gussets that don't fit at all are listed in the warnings. They're left off where they'd clash: on the side of a surface's end rib that faces the surface (across the hinge gap), and between ribs too close together for them.
- **Servo bays**: for each surface with a bay, a tray between two of the wing's own ribs where the servo fits between them (otherwise a pair of full ribs, hard points, a servo's width apart), a ply mount plate between them with a cut-out for the servo and pilot holes for its screws, and a lead hole through every rib from the bay in to the root, in a strip just behind the main spar (**Material left around servo lead holes**, under the ribs, sets how much rib is kept between them, the spar and the rib's edges: 3 mm to start with). On a fully sheeted wing the hatch in the lower sheeting is a part too, with two rails to screw it to. Warnings say if the servo is too tall for the wing there (use a smaller servo, lay it on its side, or move it out to where the wing is deeper), if there's no room between the spar and the hinge, if a rib is too thin for the lead hole (the hole is left out), or if two bays overlap. A surface's bay is set under **Control surfaces** (below); an air brake's in the wing editor, with the brake.
- **Air brakes**: for a blade brake, a box between its two ribs (full ribs, hard points): a ply wall along the front and the back from skin to skin, and the blades as ply parts with lattice slots cut through them to lighten them. A spoiler gets its plate (with its hinge line engraved) and a ply frame under the top surface with the opening for it, and hinge tape in the cut list. The slots (one per blade, a hair wider than the blade) or the opening are drawn in the plan as the cut-outs to make in the covering or sheeting; the sheeting templates themselves have no holes in them, so cut the openings from the plan.
- **Control surfaces**: for the surfaces drawn in the wing editor, first each surface's own settings: its **hinge gap**, and its **servo bay** (on unless you untick it). Choose the **Servo**, a generic class or a Hitec model (the thin wing servos grouped on their own; Automatic suits the model's weight), how it's **Mounted** (upright; on its side, which needs its width of depth rather than its height; or flat, in the wing's plane, which needs only its thickness but a bay as wide as its lugs: any servo can lie flat, though the thin wing servos are made for it). The Hitec models are sized from Hitec's dimensioned drawings, and the 3D view draws each servo to them: case, lugs, output boss and lead, and the **Bay centre**, measured from the surface's inner end along it (empty for a third of the way out along the surface). The hint gives the servo's weight, torque and the room it takes installed. Then the hinges, on every surface: a bevelled hinge line with Mylar tabs. Set the widths of the hinge spar on each side of the hinge (the fixed part's, square; the surface's, bevelled to a point at the centre line; 13 mm each to start with), their material, the Mylar roll's width (25 mm as standard, across the hinge, half into each spar) and the length each tab is cut along the hinge (25 mm to start with), the Mylar's thickness (0.2 mm), the most the tabs may be apart and how far in from the surface's ends the end ones go. Each surface gets a full rib at both ends; the ribs between stop at the fixed hinge spar, and the surface has its own ribs behind its hinge spar, an end rib at each end a gap clear of the wing's. A surface whose hinge is at the trailing edge stock's front face (within 3 mm) is the stock itself: no hinge spar or ribs of its own, just the stock cut to length with its front face bevelled, which the cut list says. Warnings say if a hinge line is too far forward (into the main spar) or back (into the trailing edge stock), if the tabs go deeper than a hinge spar is wide, or if the bevel would take nearly all of the surface's hinge spar.
- **Dihedral braces**: ply plates on both faces of the spar at every dihedral break, shaped to the dihedral angle with the break engraved on, the face grain run along the brace as a whole (half way between its two arms). Each brace has its own thickness, its row showing what the bending at its break needs; left blank, it's the next standard ply above that. The loads chart marks each brace (B1, B2...) on its margin line, with a dashed line at each break on the moment chart; a brace whose thickness falls short of the need gets a warning, and a brace with no depth at its break isn't built and is flagged.
- **Materials**: three grades of balsa, spruce, basswood, birch and lite ply, pultruded carbon strip, rod and tube, and hardwood dowel. Balsa varies two to three times from sheet to sheet, so weigh your wood and pick the grade it matches.

### Foam-core wings

A foam-core wing is a hot-wire cut core for each panel (EPS or XPS foam), skinned top and bottom with obechi veneer or balsa sheet, with no spar and no ribs: the skins carry the bending and the core the shear. The edge stock, the finish over the skins, the control-surface hinges, servo bays, air brakes, dihedral braces and design loads are set as for a built-up wing; the spar, covering, stringers, ribs and gussets give way to:

- **Foam core and skins**: the foam (EPS at 15, 20 or 30 kg/m³, or XPS "blue foam"), the skin material and thickness (obechi veneer is usually 0.6 mm; balsa 1, 1.5 or 2 mm), the size of the sheets it comes in (blank for the usual: obechi about 300 mm by 1 m, balsa 100 by 915 mm), the glue for the skins in g/m² of skin (100 g/m² by default, for the weight), and whether the wing is one piece or two halves.
- **Finish**: what goes over the skins (film, glass or carbon cloth with epoxy, and so on). Glass or carbon cloth with epoxy counts in the check as a stiffer skin.

The check takes the bending in the skins, so the margins are **skin crushing**, **skin wrinkling** (the compressed skin buckling into the foam, which a denser foam or a stiffer skin resists) and **core shear** (the foam carrying the shear across the whole chord). There's no rib spacing to check. Warnings say what's short, where, and what would help.

The weight is the foam core by its volume, the skins and their glue, the edge stock, hinge spars and the rest as on a built-up wing, the finish over the skins, and the servos.

Control surfaces are cut from the core after skinning: the core is cut and skinned whole, then each surface is sawn off along its hinge line and both cut faces capped with the hinge spars set under **Control surface hinges**. The hinge line is engraved on the hot-wire templates where it reaches the panel's ends. Servo bays and air brakes are pockets in the core: the plan outlines them, and each gets a routing template.

The 3D view shows the core as a solid between the skins (layer **Foam core**; turn it off to see the servos and pockets in it), each control surface's core with the surface, and the section cut fills the core too. The structure card shows the foam, the number of cores and the skins in place of the covering and ribs.

### Reading the results

The structure card lists the control surfaces built, the servo bays, whether crow is available or on, and the air brakes built. The wing weight includes the servos, and the root bending moment is a little lower for them: each servo's weight relieves the spar, in proportion to how far out it sits.

The plan has a **Layers** menu (top left) like the 3D view's, to hide the sheeting, edge stock, spar, stringers, ribs, control surfaces, hinge spars and tabs, servos (with their leads' routes), servo plates and hatches, or air brakes; it lists only those the wing has. The plan shades bays amber where ribs are too far apart and red where the spar, web or sheeting is short of strength in itself; closer ribs only fix the first. A warning says which part, where, by how much and what would help, including roughly the cap thickness needed. Sheeted areas are hatched.

The **Part** card shows one piece of the wing at a time on its own, the one selected in the plan, the 3D view or its own list. A piece cut from sheet (a rib, gusset, web, brace, servo plate, a panel's sheet) is drawn flat as it's cut, with its holes and engraved lines, its size, thickness, material, how many to cut and its notes; a piece that isn't gives its material and length, and is drawn dimensioned: a spar cap, the leading or trailing edge, a stringer or a hinge spar in cross-section at mid-span (hatched and labelled Section), a cap strip as its side profile on the rib, a foam core as the end of the block it's cut from (the block's size, with the core's section at each end inside it), and a servo from the side, as in its maker's drawing (across its lugs, up to the lugs and up to the horn seat). Under that it's drawn in 3D as it sits in the wing, the same way round as the 3D view shows the wing (a part that isn't drawn in the wing, in a three-quarter view): drag to turn it, right-drag to move it; − + Fit in the corner (F, + and − while it has focus) zoom and fit it without turning it, and the house top right turns it back to the view it opened in. Step through with the arrows, or open the list by its name: every part with a thumbnail of its shape and its size, grouped as in the export, then the pieces that aren't cut from sheet. A part chosen from the list shows the first of its pieces in 3D (a gusset group has many); one not drawn in the wing, such as a hatch or a template, is shown as a solid of its thickness.

The **Loads** card plots the bending moment along the span, and below it the margin of safety for each failure mode (spar in compression and tension, sheeting, shear web, web buckling, buckling between ribs), one coloured line each with a legend giving its lowest margin. The lowest of all is marked with a dot, and its line and legend entry are heavier. The shaded band below 0 is short of strength; margins above +3 are drawn at +3. Each chart has the key to its lines above it and its readout under it: the moment at the root rib and each mode's lowest margin along the span, or, while you point at either chart, every curve's value at that place. The ⓘ button beside the expand button shows the notes on what the charts count. Beside the Part card the Loads card takes the same height, its charts filling what's left. The button in the corner opens both charts in a large panel over the page; the same corner takes them back to the card.

The **3D** view shows the built-up half wing, built from the same geometry as the cut parts, without the covering (film, tissue or cloth isn't drawn): sheeting (a hard point's too) and cap strips, ribs, gussets and braces, a rounded tip's block, spar, shear webs, edge stock, stringers and control surfaces with their hinge spars and Mylar tabs (shown undeflected), with twist and dihedral true to scale.

- Drag to turn, right-drag to move, scroll to zoom; − + Fit in the bottom-right corner.
- Click the view cube (Top, Bottom, Front, Back, Root, Tip) to swing round to that view. The house beside it goes back to the starting angle, and the button under it switches between orthographic (the default, which makes the cube's views true elevations) and perspective.
- **Layers** (top left), listing only what this wing has: show **Both halves** for the whole wing, hide **Control surfaces** (each surface's ribs, its own hinge spar and trailing edge) and **Hinge spars** (the fixed ones, with the Mylar tabs) separately, **Sheeting**, **Cap strips**, **Gussets** and **Dihedral braces** each on their own, hide **Servos** (dark, with the output boss and lead) and **Servo plates** separately, hide **Air brakes** (the box walls, and the blades or spoiler plate drawn raised, in grey), hide layers to see inside, and turn **Outlines** (the sharp edges of every part) on or off.
- Click any piece to select it and show it on its own in the **Part** card: a rib, gusset, web, brace, a panel's top or bottom sheet, a spar cap, the leading edge, a stretch of trailing edge, a stringer, a hinge spar, a cap strip, a servo or its plate, an air brake's wall or blade, a Mylar tab, the tip block or a foam core. The selected piece is highlighted; selecting a part from the plan or the list highlights every piece cut as it.
- **Section** cuts the wing square to the span: everything outboard is hidden and the cut face is filled, coloured by part. A bar along the bottom places the cut: drag along it (the ticks are the ribs, each named as on the plan unless its name would overlap another, the two either side of the cut highlighted and named first; the cut is kept off the ribs themselves), and it shows the distance from the root rib and the bay. **Flip** keeps the outboard part instead, **Look at cut** swings round to face it, and × closes the section.

## Export

**Export…** in the structure editor offers four downloads (five for a foam-core wing):

- **Parts (DXF)**: every part laid out flat in rows no longer than the sheet length you set, grouped by stock (material and thickness), each group titled. Grain runs left to right along the sheet; shear webs are turned so theirs runs up the web. One copy per piece needed, or one of each.
- **Plan (DXF)**: full size, to build over: the wing outline, edge stock, spar, stringers and every rib at its true thickness, with rib names and the sheeted areas hatched, and on a HINGE layer each control surface's hinge spars, its ends and every Mylar tab, on a BRAKE layer each air brake's box (or a spoiler's frame), the slots or opening it needs in the covering and a spoiler's hinge line, and on a SERVO layer each servo bay's hatch opening, the servo (case and lug span) and the route its lead takes to the root. The surfaces' own ribs are named behind the trailing edge. Right half, left half (mirrored) or both.
- **Cut list (CSV)**: every part with quantity, stock and size, and every length of strip, with how many lengths of edge stock to buy. Each control surface's hinge spars are listed with their depth, the bevel (top and bottom, from square) and the number of tab slots; the surfaces' ribs (A1-1, A1-2 …), the Mylar tabs, the servo mount plates (SB1 …) and, on a sheeted wing, the hatches (SB1-H) and their rails are parts or strips like any other. The air brakes' box walls, blades, plates and frames are parts too (AB1-F, AB1-R, AB1-B for a blade brake; AB1-P and AB1-F for a spoiler), with a spoiler's hinge tape in the strip list. Choose whether the servo plates and hatches, and the air brake parts, go in the parts DXF in the export dialogue.
- **Hot-wire templates (DXF)**, for a foam-core wing: a pair of templates for each panel's core, the inner end's beside the outer end's (HW1-I and HW1-O, and so on root outward), each the core's section between the skins from the back of the leading edge stock to the front of the trailing edge stock, with the panel's twist built in. Both templates of a pair are drawn in one frame, with an engraved **level line** and **vertical mark** that are the same line on both: set them on the block with the level lines the same height above its base and the marks on one line square across it, and the core comes out swept and twisted as designed. Each is bigger than the core all round by half the **wire kerf** (1 mm by default, set under **Hot wire** in the export dialogue), so the core comes out true size; measure the kerf from a test cut in the same foam at the same heat and speed. Ticks every tenth of the chord, top and bottom, help two people call out the wire's progress at each end; the spar cap slots and any shear web are engraved, and so is the hinge line of any control surface on that panel. The wire's **lead-in** at the trailing edge and **lead-out** at the front are drawn on the notes layer. One pair cuts both halves: turn the block over, and the templates with it. The templates also go in the parts DXF and the cut list, and print with the PDFs.
- **Printed templates (PDF, 1:1)**: the parts (true size, no kerf allowance) or the plan, to build over or cut round by hand. On A4, A3, Letter or Tabloid the drawing is split across as few sheets as possible, overlapping by 10 mm, each named (A1, A2 …, rows down the drawing) with its neighbours marked on its edges. Or one sheet sized to the drawing, for a plotter or print shop. Every sheet has a title block and a 50 mm bar to check it printed at 100%.

The DXF is AutoCAD R12 in millimetres, which nearly all laser, router and CAD software reads, as closed polylines or (for older software) separate lines. Layers are cut (red), engrave (blue) and notes (grey), with names you can change. Labels are single-stroke vector lines in a Hershey font, so they engrave the same in any software. A part too small to carry a label at least 2 mm high (a corner gusset, say) has it engraved on the sheet just below it instead, so you can tell the pieces apart as you pick them out.

A foam-core wing's cut list adds the foam blocks to buy (big enough for both ends of each core, swept and twisted, with 10 mm all round) and the skin sheets: each skin (K1, K2 …, top and bottom for each panel, developed from the section; cut oversize and trim) says how many sheets are joined edge to edge across its width, and a line totals the sheets to buy. The servo and air brake pockets have routing templates (SB1-T, AB1-T …): a plate with the pocket's true size cut out, to follow with a bearing-guided cutter or enlarged by your guide bush's offset, with how deep to rout in the notes. A shear web is one part per panel (W1 …), the depth between the caps at each end.

Lightening holes and jig tabs change the parts themselves, so they're set in the structure editor under **Ribs**; the export dialogue only says which are on.

- **Laser**: every cut moves half the kerf into the waste, so parts come out true size (set the kerf to 0 if your laser software compensates).
- **CNC router**: dogbones in every inside corner, and optionally the cutter-radius offset if your CAM doesn't apply it. Corners too tight for the cutter are listed.

Export options are saved with the project, so a re-export comes out the same.
