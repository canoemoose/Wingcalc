# Wingcalc v8: the wing, finished — scope

First of three bodies of work towards designing a whole model:

1. **v8, the wing, finished** (this file): everything that works on one wing.
2. **v9, the rest of the plane** (`docs/scope-v9.md`): tail, foreplane, fins, fuselage, layout, stability.
3. **v10, make it fly** (`docs/scope-v10.md`): power, electronics, mass and CG, flight loads, performance.

Each body depends only on the ones before it, and each ends with something usable. Draft, not started.

## Objective

Fix and round off the structure editor from Sam's feedback (2026-10-16). Then add what a finished wing needs: control surfaces with their hinges and servo bays, foam-core construction, and a drawn section editor. The tail surfaces in v9 reuse the control-surface and servo work unchanged.

## Decisions

- **Control surfaces**: ailerons and flaps, either as separate surfaces or mixed (flaperons). **Crow** braking only when ailerons and flaps are separate surfaces.
- **Air brakes**: lattice brakes or pop-up spoilers, as physical parts.
- **Hinges**: the default is a bevelled hinge line with Mylar tabs slotted at the centre line.
- **Servo bays are modelled; pushrods are not.** Servo positions count in the wing's weight (see the assumptions below).
- **Foam-core wings** with obechi or balsa skins, alongside built-up construction.
- **Custom sections** get an "Advanced" mode: a 2D CAD-like section editor.

### Assumptions (to confirm)

- Servo and bay mass adds to the wing's estimated weight. The servos' position along the span also relieves spar bending (inertia relief), so it counts in the structure check too.
- "Lattice" air brakes are Schempp-Hirth style: lattice or slotted blades that rise vertically out of a box in the wing. Pop-up spoilers are a hinged plate on the top surface.

## Part A: fixes and tweaks (about ½ round)

1. **Sam's three reports**: manual rib spacing, gussets, and ribs clashing with the TE. Each one waits on his answers (`docs/backlog.md`, Questions for Sam).
2. **Leading-edge types**: carbon rod, dowel and none (a sheeted nose), alongside balsa stock. Round stock sits in a notch or hole in each rib. Add fit, weight and cut list for each type.
   - [x] Done. `le.type` ('balsa' default, so older saves are unchanged; 'rod', 'dowel', 'none') and `le.diameter` in `defaultStructure()`. Round stock forms the nose and sits in a half-round notch at the front of each rib (notch only, no hole option), ribs stopping at its centre line; it fits if its rear half is inside the section at every rib, with the nose taken as rounded ahead of 2% chord (the coordinate polyline makes a wedge nose that no circle fits). Sizes: carbon rod 1.5–8 mm in 1 m lengths, hardwood dowel 3–10 mm in 915 mm lengths (new materials `carbon-rod`, `hardwood` at 700 kg/m³). 'none' runs the ribs to the nose and warns on an open wing. LE corner gussets only against balsa stock.
3. **Edge-stock messages**: rewrite "… is too small to carve to the section: about N mm short" (`structure.js`) in a builder's terms, and show the shortfall on the section drawing.
   - [x] Done. The warning now names the edge, the stock, where it's worst (root rib, a panel end or the tip rib), what goes wrong when carved and what to use instead. The editor's "Leading and trailing edges" section draws the fit where it's worst whenever it doesn't fit (`EdgeFit.vue`, geometry from `fitDrawing` in `edgeStock.js`), red where the stock falls short or a rod stands proud. There was no section drawing in the structure editor to add it to, so it's a new small figure there rather than an overlay on `SectionView`/`PartPreview`. The thin-at-the-TE warning and rib note lost their "TE"/"cut" jargon too.
4. **Margin chart legend**: the "Lowest margin of safety" panel in `LoadsChart.vue` draws one line per failure mode with no labels.
   - [x] Done. In fact it drew a single lowest-margin line; it now draws one line per mode (colour fixed per mode, categorical palette validated with the dataviz checker, light and dark steps as `--wc-series-*`), a legend below with each mode's lowest margin, the governing one heavier, and a native tooltip per line.
5. **Coverings**: make the covering an explicit, visible choice rather than an unstated assumption of film over open bays. Options: heat-shrink film (light and heavy), fabric film, tissue and dope, and glass and epoxy over sheeting. Each has a weight per area, and stiffness where it counts (torsion in a D-box or sheeted wing). The editor says what the check assumes, and the methods doc says how covering enters the sums.
   - [x] Done. `covering.finish` (default 'film', heat-shrink film at 90 g/m²; older saves migrate to it): margins on every existing test and the default wing are unchanged, only the weight estimate grows (by about 55 g on the default wing, which stays inside the existing plausibility test). Glass and epoxy counts in bending and sheet buckling (as a laminate on the sheeting); torsion isn't checked, so it gets no torsion credit. Per-area figures are typical published ones taken at the heavy end, not measured: worth Sam's eye. Covering doesn't yet change the "most the ribs may be apart" default (75 mm, set for film); tissue might want closer.

## Part B: wing features (about 3–3½ rounds)

6. **Control surfaces and hinges** (1 round). Per panel: the hinge line as a chord fraction (it may taper) over a span range. Each surface has a role (aileron, flap, flaperon) and a deflection range. Rib split at the hinge; hinge spars on both sides with a centre slot for the Mylar tabs; bevel angle from the maximum deflection; the gap. Tab count and spacing along the hinge, and tabs in the cut list. Separate parts for each moving surface, in the plan, 3D and DXF. XFOIL's flap deflection gives CLmax and stall speed with flap and crow. The mechanism doesn't know which surface it's on, so v9's elevators, rudders, elevons and ruddervators use it unchanged.
   - [x] Done, including (in a later round) the XFOIL flap deflection: CLmax, stall speed and stall onset for clean, flaps and crow, under Stall speed (`src/lib/flaps.js`; methods "Flaps and crow"). Decisions: deflected polars by GDES FLAP at the clean polar's Re, applied as increments on the clean section; thin-aerofoil flap theory with an empirical correction as the fallback; stalled flapped stations hold their Clmax for up to 3° (progressive stall) so a partial-span flap isn't penalised by the first-station rule; the clean wing stays the headline and the structure's case; crow offered when it's on. `input.controls = { surfaces, crow }` (`defaultControls()`; older saves get none). A surface is `{ id, role, panel (id), from, to (m from the panel's inner end), hingeIn, hingeOut (chord fractions), up, down (°), gap }`. **One panel per surface**, not a free span range: a hinge has to be straight, and across a dihedral break or a kink in the edges it can't be; panel-relative ends also follow the panel when inboard panels change. Placement, bevel, tabs and crow are in `src/lib/controlSurfaces.js`, generic over a `wingSampler`, so v9 can reuse it; the built-up hinge is `surfaceLayouts` in `structure.js`, and parts in `ribs.js`.
     - Hinge: fixed hinge spar (default 8 mm, square) ahead of the gap, moving hinge spar (10 mm) behind it bevelled to a point at the centre line; both slotted for Mylar tabs (12 × 15 mm, 0.2 mm, at most 75 mm apart, end ones 20 mm in). Bevel from the throws and gap, exact geometry about the mid-gap, no extra allowance (worth Sam's eye: builders often just bevel to the full throw). Defaults on adding: 75% chord hinge, aileron 20° up / 15° down, flap 5° / 45°, flaperon 20° / 30°, 1 mm gap.
     - Ends: a full rib (hard point) at each end; the moving surface stops a gap short with its own end ribs. Ribs between are split (`split` on the fixed part, group `surfaces` for the moving parts, named A1-1 …). Tabs are part H1 (group `hinges`, Mylar); both hinge spars are cut list strips with bevel and slot notes. All weighed; the hinge spars don't count in bending, and hinge moments aren't checked.
     - Shown in the plan editor (add from the command bar on the selected panel, select, Delete), inspector (`ControlSurfaceEditor.vue`, in the inputs column and the wing editor), structure plan, 3D (own layer; trailing edge broken at each surface), parts and plan DXF (HINGE layer), cut list and thumbnails. Crow: a flag with aileron-up and flap-down settings, available only with separate ailerons and flaps and no flaperons.
     - Deferred: dragging hinge ends in the plan; sheeting templates on a fully sheeted wing aren't split at the hinge; the trailing edge stock's cut-list length isn't split at the surfaces' ends; surfaces aren't drawn deflected in 3D; hinge moments and servo sizing (step 8).
7. **Air brakes** (½ round). Lattice blade brakes in a box between ribs, and pop-up spoilers as a hinged top-surface plate. Placement along the span, a cut-out in the sheeting, a box or mounts, and parts. Their drag is used later, in the performance work in v9 and v10.
   - [x] Done. `controls.brakes` (`defaultControls()`; older saves get none) holds `{ id, type ('blade' | 'spoiler'), panel (id), at (centre, m from the panel's inner end), length, x (chord fraction of the centre), chord (box width or plate chord), height, blades, surface ('top' | 'both'), angle }`; both kinds' fields are always saved so changing type loses nothing. Placement is in `src/lib/airBrakes.js` (generic over a wing sampler, like control surfaces), the built box and its checks are `brakeLayouts` in `structure.js`, parts in `ribs.js`. **One panel per brake**, as for surfaces.
     - Blade brake: a box between two hard-point ribs (1.5 mm clear of the blade length each end), a 1 mm ply wall along the front and back from skin to skin, blades as ply parts (single, or several side by side as the lattice; through the top or top and bottom) with lattice cut-outs as lightening slots, a slot per blade in the covering. Spoiler: a 1.5 mm ply plate hinged on its front edge, a ply frame under the top surface with the opening (also the covering cut-out), hinge tape in the cut list.
     - Fit and clash checks, each a warning (the brake is still built): depth for the blades retracted (height + 2 mm, double + 4 mm for both skins), the box crossing the main spar, the edge stock, a control surface's hinge spar alongside it, a servo bay in plan, or the lead holes of an outer bay. Ribs on another brake's ribs: not built ('overlap').
     - **A servo bay per brake by default**, through the same mechanism: bays are keyed `'b<id>'` in `controls.bays`, `placeBays` takes brake hosts as well as surfaces (`brakeHosts`), and the bay goes beside the box on the inboard side sharing its rib (outboard if that's off the panel), with the servo between the leads' strip and the trailing edge stock. Bay and servo weight and the inertia relief are automatic.
     - Shown in: the wing editor's plan (drawn as the box or plate; add from the bar, select, Delete), the inspector (`AirBrakeEditor.vue` below the control surfaces, in the inputs column and the wing editor), the structure plan, the 3D view (Air brakes layer: box walls, blades or the spoiler plate drawn raised), parts and plan DXF (BRAKE layer), cut list, export dialogue and the plan thumbnail.
     - **Drag**: not computed. The brakes' dimensions are all in `controls.brakes` for the performance work in v9 (glide polar with air brakes) and v10 to take a drag increment from.
     - Deferred: linkage and guides, holes in the sheeting templates, dragging in the plan, the 3D section cut and rib thumbnails (see `docs/backlog.md`).
8. **Servo bays** (½ round). A servo catalogue by class (sub-micro, micro, mini, standard), with case size, mounting-lug size, mass and torque. A bay sits between ribs (a hard point at each end), with a mount plate or rails, a hatch in the sheeting and a lead route through the ribs. One bay per control surface or air brake by default. Servo mass goes into the wing weight and the spar loads. Optional: compare the servo's torque with the hinge moment.
   - [x] Done for control surfaces and, since step 7, air brakes (a bay beside each brake); the hinge moment comparison is left for later (below). `controls.bays` holds only the settings that differ from the defaults, `{ surface (id), enabled: false?, class?, at? }`; every placed surface has a bay unless `enabled` is false, so older saves and new surfaces get one automatically (`bayOf`, `setBay` in `src/lib/servos.js`). Catalogue (`SERVOS`, generic sizes: sub-micro, micro, mini, standard) and its basis are in `docs/methods.md`; torque is the 4.8 V figure rounded down, and is shown but not checked.
     - Placement: centred on the surface unless `at` (m from the panel's inner end) says otherwise; the ribs either side (hard points, named like any other, via the same `extra` mechanism as the surfaces' end ribs) are a servo's width plus 1.5 mm each side apart. Class defaults from the all-up mass (up to 0.6 kg sub-micro, 2.5 micro, 6 mini, then standard).
     - Built bay (`bayLayouts` in `structure.js`): the servo stands shaft vertical, lugs along the chord, between the leads' strip behind the main spar and the fixed hinge spar, in the deepest place there; a ply mount plate between the rib faces with a case cut-out and four pilot holes (part SB1 …, group `servos`); the hatch is the plate's outline in the lower surface, a part (SB1-H) with two spruce rails in the cut list only on a fully sheeted wing, otherwise a plan outline and a note to cut the covering. Lead holes: one per bay in every rib from the bay's inner rib to the root, in a strip behind the spar (outermost bay nearest it), each kept MIN_MATERIAL from rib edges and notches, sliding aft up to 20 mm, or left out with a note and warning. Label and lightening holes keep clear of them.
     - Weight: servos are in `weight.total` (and `weight.servos`), per half wing times the number of halves; plates, hatches, rails are weighed as parts.
     - **Assumption stated, as asked:** "servo locations factored into wing loading" is taken as inertia relief. Each servo is a point mass on the half wing, pulling down n × m × g at its station, so it lowers the shear inboard of it and the bending moment by that force times its distance from the section, at both positive and negative load factor. Only bays that are built count. The wing's own structure is still ignored (safe side).
     - Shown in: the inspector and wing editor (a Servo bay block in each control surface's section), the structure plan (hatch, servo, lead route), the 3D view (Servo bays layer: servo and plate), parts and plan DXF (SERVO layer), cut list, export dialogue (servo plates and hatches). Not in the thumbnails beyond the lead holes in the ribs, and not in the 3D section cut.
     - Deferred: hinge moment against servo torque (needs a top speed and horn geometry, neither modelled); servos on their sides for thin wings (a default micro servo, 24.5 mm with headroom, won't fit a wing under about 15% thick behind the spar, so the default wing warns); pushrod and horn; the lead strip is sized for all bays so one bay's rear limit can be hit before it needs to; a bay doesn't check for stringers or the sheeting's seams crossing its hatch; a bay can't be dragged in the plan.
9. **Foam-core construction** (1 round). A wing construction type alongside built-up. Foam (EPS and XPS densities), obechi or balsa veneer skins, spar caps under the skins (carbon or spruce), and hardwood or balsa leading and trailing edges. Structure check as a sandwich: the skins and caps carry bending, plus skin buckling. Exports: hot-wire templates per panel (root and tip, kerf allowance, lead-in, alignment marks), a skin cut list, and pockets for servos and brakes. Control surfaces are cut from the core at the hinge line (step 6).
   - [x] Done. `structure.construction` ('built-up' default, so older saves are unchanged; 'foam') and `structure.foam` (`defaultFoam()` in `src/lib/foam.js`: `core`, `skin { material, thickness, sheetWidth, sheetLength }`, `glue` kg/m², `caps { material, width, thickness }`, `web { enabled, material, thickness }`), both filled in by `fillDefaults`. The built-up settings are kept while foam is chosen, and the edges, finish, hinges, braces, loads, wing halves and spar position are shared. Export options gain `wireKerf` (1 mm) and `leadIn` (20 mm) and the part groups `templates`, `skins` (off) and `pockets`.
     - Decision: a foam wing is laid out as its equivalent fully sheeted built-up wing with a caps spar and no ribs (`foamStructure`), so the caps under the skins, the skins in bending, hinge spars, servo bays, air brakes, 3D and plan all reuse the built-up code. `analyseStructure` swaps the rib modes for **skin wrinkling** (σ = 0.5 (E<sub>f</sub> E<sub>c</sub> G<sub>c</sub>)<sup>1/3</sup>) and **core shear** (V / ((w + d) d), the skins' share left out), or the web's shear when there is one; no rib spacing. `a.ribs` is empty and `a.cores` lists the panels.
     - Materials (cited in methods): EPS 15, 20, 30 and XPS (EN 13163/13164 indicative, conservative), obechi veneer (Wood Handbook, taken down), carbon tow (wet laid), template stock (weightless). Skins: obechi or balsa; caps: carbon strip, carbon tow or spruce; edges reuse the edge stock. Assumed sheet sizes: obechi 300 × 1000 mm, balsa 100 × 915 mm (settable). Glue 100 g/m² of skin (settable).
     - Templates (`src/lib/foamParts.js`): a pair per panel (HW1-I, HW1-O …), the core between the skins from the LE stock to the TE stock, sweep and twist in one frame with a shared level line and vertical mark, offset outward by half the wire kerf, chord ticks every tenth, cap and web slots and hinge lines engraved, lead-in and lead-out on the notes layer, bevel note at dihedral joints. Their own DXF download (pairs side by side), PDF, and in the parts DXF. **Control surfaces are a cut line, not a separate core**: a surface rarely spans the whole panel, so separate cores would split the panel at its ends, and a core skinned whole keeps the surface true; it's sawn off after skinning and faced with the hinge spars.
     - Skins K1 … per panel and surface (developed, approximate), with sheets across and along and a sheets-to-buy line; foam blocks in the cut list; a shear web part per panel; routing templates for servo and brake pockets (SB1-T, AB1-T …, true-size opening, depth noted). Weight: core volume × density, skins plus glue (no 10% on them), the rest as built-up, finish, servos.
     - Shown in: the structure editor (Construction at the top; Foam core and skins, Spar caps, Finish), the structure card (foam, cores, skins), the loads chart (foam mode names, wrinkling and core shear in the rib modes' colour slots), the 3D view (Foam core layer, a surface's core with the surface, core in the section cut), the 3D thumbnail (the cores' end faces), the export dialogue (Hot wire section, templates download and PDF).
     - Deferred (`docs/backlog.md`): G-code and four-axis CNC, the skins' share of the shear, slots and pockets in the 3D core, curved planforms between templates, balsa skins' cross-grain stiffness in wrinkling, the lead channel.
10. **Section editor** (½–1 round). "Advanced" on a custom section opens a 2D editor in the house style (`StageZoom`, command bar, no tool rail). It edits spline control points on the upper and lower surfaces, starting from any section. Readouts for thickness, camber and leading-edge radius, plus a curvature check. XFOIL runs on the result as for any other section. The drawn section is saved with the project.

Total: about 4 rounds.
